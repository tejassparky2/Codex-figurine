# What the sprint starts with

Owner-approved art and its documented overrides define the finish. Register the art to an upright mesh, sample the original UV texture as evidence, infer broad materials, trace visible details with a depth-tested face raster, and resolve physical contacts using geometry. Cone-thickness locks protected a glasses frame and separated a thumb from trousers on the drink figurine. Keep those locks through the last contour split. Inspect the exported finish independently of the four-head project. Tilting may cause one tool to require two different pigments in one layer.

The wedding baseline has several distinct checkpoints. A successful archive replay establishes deterministic paint and geometry. It cannot establish that a cuff is the intended color. Claude measured the front audit moving from 0.859% to 0.932% disagreement despite 1,669 high-confidence edit decisions. Targeted contact corrections plus protected contour subdivision reached 0.695%, with a separately fitted camera. Shorter interface length measures contour complexity. A calibrated agreement score measures only visible areas where the referee can classify the art.

The gate fits a silhouette camera, allows capped local alignment, classifies approved pixels against anchors and reports interior disagreements and boundary distances. Self-tests plant known visible label defects. The judge portrait differs from the generating pose; unknown back surfaces, white-on-white silhouettes, shadowed cream/skin, low-confidence anchors and narrow details limit interpretation. The drink judge still needs owner-approved overrides reflected in its image. Both judges are practice only.

Code is authoritative for what the gate executed. Its verdict allows thresholds and excludes pixels; a reported PASS is evidence under those rules, rather than literal color equality at every pixel. Owner acceptance, slicer paths and physical print checks require their own evidence. No prior wedding candidate is certified clean by the current referee.

One naming discrepancy matters for the scoreboard. Its “clear disagreement” column uses `hard_mismatch_pct`, which includes out-of-band pixels from close-call blobs. The FAIL threshold instead uses the pixel sum of firm blobs after size filtering and the largest firm blob. The tables here follow the scoreboard for comparability. `silhouette_iou` can also include an accepted optical-flow warp, so the two arms can report different IoU even with identical geometry and camera.
